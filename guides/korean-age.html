How Age Is Counted in Korea

You may hear three different ages for the same person in Korea. Each one counts in a different way.

The three counts

  • International age (man nai): you are 0 at birth and gain a year on each birthday.
  • Year age (yeon nai): the current year minus your birth year.
  • Traditional age (seneun nai): you are 1 at birth and gain a year every January 1.

A worked example

Take someone born on December 10, 2000, and look at March 1, 2026. Their birthday has not come yet that year, so their international age is 25. Their year age is 2026 − 2000 = 26. Their traditional age is 27.

On December 10, 2026, only the international age changes, to 26. On January 1, 2027, the year age and traditional age both go up by one.

What changed in 2023

On June 28, 2023, revised laws confirmed that international age is the default in legal and administrative matters. This did not make year age disappear. Some areas still go by birth year, including buying alcohol and tobacco and military service duties. School entry also follows its own year-based rule. The change did not ban people from using other counts in conversation.

Alcohol and tobacco

Under the Youth Protection Act, the age line is passed on January 1 of the year in which you turn 19. Two people born in the same year pass it on the same day, whatever their birthdays. Shops may ask for ID.

In conversation

Age affects speech level and what people call each other, so you may be asked about it early. Many people ask for your birth year: Myeot nyeonsaeng-ieyo? For 1998 you can answer Gu-pal nyeonsaeng-ieyo (“born in ’98”).
