How Korean Names Work

Family name first

A Korean name starts with the family name. In Kim Min-seo (김민서), Kim is the family name and Min-seo is the given name. A typical full name has three syllables: one for the family name and two for the given name. There are also one-syllable given names and two-syllable family names.

Family names

Kim (김), Lee (이) and Park (박) are the three most common family names. Their English spellings vary. For example, 이 may be written Lee, Yi or Rhee. Use the spelling that the person uses. By custom, people keep their own family name when they marry.

Hanja and meaning

Many given names are based on Hanja, which are Chinese characters used in Korean. One Hangul syllable can match many different characters. This means two people called 지우 can have names with different meanings. You cannot tell the meaning from Hangul alone.

Native Korean names

Some names come from native Korean words and have no Hanja. Examples are 하늘 (Ha-neul, “sky”), 바다 (Ba-da, “sea”) and 이슬 (I-seul, “dew”).

Using a name politely

Calling someone by the given name alone can sound too familiar if you are not close. Adding -ssi to the full name or given name is a common polite form among equals. At work, people are often addressed by job title. When unsure, ask what the person prefers.

About chosen Korean names

A Korean name made for fun is a nickname. It does not change your legal name, and our results are not traditional naming advice. Ask a Korean speaker how a name sounds today before you use it widely.
