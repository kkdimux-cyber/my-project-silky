How to Write a Foreign Name in Hangul

Sound comes before spelling

To write a foreign name in Hangul, start with how its owner says it. The same spelling can be said differently in different languages, so it can be written differently in Korean too.

Syllable blocks

Hangul letters are grouped into blocks. Each block has a first consonant and a vowel, and may have a final consonant. When a syllable starts with a vowel sound, the silent letter ㅇ fills the first place. For example, 한 is ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ.

Sounds without an exact match

  • F and P are usually both written with ㅍ.
  • V and B are usually both written with ㅂ.
  • Z and J are usually both written with ㅈ.
  • R and L share the letter ㄹ. An L between vowels is often written as ㄹㄹ across two blocks, as in 앨리스 (Alice).
  • Extra vowels: Korean often adds ㅡ to carry a consonant that has no vowel, as in 크리스 (Chris).

Examples

  • Emma: 에마
  • Michael: 마이클
  • Grace: 그레이스

The National Institute of Korean Language publishes the rules for writing foreign words. Personal habit can differ from the rules, and the owner of a name may already have a preferred Korean spelling.

Official documents

For passports, visas, banking and phone contracts, use the name exactly as the office asks, which is usually the spelling on your official ID.
